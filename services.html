01 Comprehensive eye & vision exams

Regular eye exams are an invaluable tool for keeping eyes healthy by detecting and preventing disease. Some diseases develop slowly without causing pain or vision loss, and early detection can reduce the risk of further harm and allow for a choice of treatment options. The American Optometric Association recommends yearly or every-other-year exams, depending on whether you are at risk. We care for adults and children, and the whole process can take about an hour depending on your needs.

Every exam includes a refraction, slit lamp exam, eye pressure check and retinal exam. Here’s what to expect:

  • Consultation — we ask about any symptoms or blurry vision, medications, your work environment, overall health, family history and past eye conditions.
  • Vision testing — we measure how each eye sees using a distance eye chart and a reading chart (20/20 is the standard for normal vision). Depending on the results, your doctor may prescribe glasses, contacts or eye exercises.
  • Eye function testing — tests for depth perception, color vision, eye muscle ability, peripheral vision and response to light, plus how your eyes focus, move and work together.
  • Eye health evaluation — we inspect the eye and eyelids with magnification and a bright light, and measure eye pressure (tonometry), since higher pressure can be a sign of glaucoma. To see inside the eye we may dilate the pupils. Your doctor reviews the results and discusses any treatment options with you.
02 Drop-free exams & retinal imaging

In many cases, Modern Eyes provides a completely drop-free eye care experience. Ultra-wide-field retinal imaging is our standard, along with corneal topography, visual field screening, iCare tonometry (eye pressure), and other front- and back-of-the-eye health equipment.

For many patients, that means no dilating drops and a more comfortable visit.

03 Contact lens exams & fittings

Thanks to advances in optical technology, almost everyone is a candidate for contact lenses, including patients with astigmatism and those who prefer bifocal or multifocal lenses. After your eye and vision exam, your doctor will talk through your options so you can choose the type that best fits your vision needs and lifestyle. We also handle prescription management for your lenses.

  • Evaluation — we measure your refractive error (nearsightedness, farsightedness or astigmatism) to see what level of correction you need.
  • Corrective lenses — we help you decide whether contacts or glasses suit you best. Contacts come in soft or rigid gas permeable (RGP) forms, including daily disposables, extended-wear soft lenses and planned-replacement lenses changed daily, weekly, bi-weekly or monthly. Bifocal contacts are available for patients with special eye conditions.
  • Cosmetic lenses — custom lenses can camouflage a color variation or irregularity for a natural eye color, and cosmetic lenses can change your eye color.

If you have dry eye, allergies or recurring eye infections, talk with our staff first. Glasses may be the most comfortable choice for some conditions.

04 Hard-to-fit & specialty contacts

Some eye conditions make wearing contacts harder, but that doesn’t rule them out. You’ll need to meet with your eye care provider and be prescribed lenses tailored to your condition. We offer regular and custom lens services for both healthy and difficult-to-fit patients.

  • Astigmatism — custom toric lenses that stay aligned on the eye. They must not rotate, so they take longer to make and cost more than a traditional contact lens.
  • Keratoconus — gas permeable lenses help contain corneal bulging and relieve pressure on the tissue.
  • Presbyopia — bifocal or multifocal lenses, or monovision (one eye fitted for distance and the other for close-up).
  • Dry eyes — medicated eye drops can lubricate the eyes enough to make contacts more bearable; in some extreme cases a punctal occlusion is also needed.
  • Giant papillary conjunctivitis (GPC) — gas permeable lenses limit protein deposits, and medicated drops can flush out deposits and reduce inflammation.

For corneal conditions such as keratoconus, pellucid marginal degeneration or post-refractive ectasia, specialty contact lenses such as scleral or hybrid lenses may be the best option.

06 Frame style guide by face shape

Glasses say a lot about your personality and style. Here’s a general guide to which frames tend to complement each face shape. Our staff will help you find what works best for your lifestyle.

  • Square (strong jaw line, broad forehead, face width and length close to equal) — frames that make the face look longer and soften angles: slightly curved, oval-like shapes with more horizontal than vertical room.
  • Oval (balanced proportions, high cheekbones) — frames as wide as or wider than the broadest part of the face that follow your brow line. Diamond or rectangular shapes often work best.
  • Oblong (longer than it is wide) — frames with depth and a low bridge that break up the length of the face: round, deep, low-triangle shapes, or strong vertical lines.
  • Round (width and length are about the same) — angular, narrow frames that are wider than they are deep. Avoid round frames, which exaggerate the curves of the face.
  • Heart (very wide forehead, high cheekbones, narrow chin) — frames wider at the bottom to balance the chin. Light-colored and rimless frames generally work best, though aviator, butterfly and low-triangle styles also work well.
  • Triangle / base-down (narrower forehead, full cheeks, broad chin) — frames that widen at the top, with heavy color accents and detail on the upper part. Cat-eye shapes also work well.
  • Diamond (high, dramatic cheekbones, narrow eye line and jaw line; the rarest shape) — soft oval frames, or frames with detailing, distinctive brow lines, rimless or cat-eye shapes.
07 Sunglasses & UV protection

Bright, sunny days are great, but UV and glare can cause problems for your eyes, from “snowblindness” to irreversible disorders that threaten your eyesight. We can help you choose the right pair.

  • What are UV rays? UV stands for ultraviolet, a band of light invisible to the eye. UVA and UVB rays can both reach the eye and potentially damage it.
  • What does UV do to unprotected eyes? It can cloud the proteins inside the lens (cataracts, which can only be removed, not reversed), cause retinal damage and changes in eye tissue, and cause a temporary “sunburn” of the cornea called photokeratitis.
  • How do I know my sunglasses protect? Choose glasses that block at least 99 percent of UVA and UVB rays. Look for a “UV 400” label. To cut glare, choose lenses that block 75 to 90 percent of visible light.
  • What are polarized lenses? They filter the glare that bounces up from horizontal surfaces, and are recommended for boating, fishing, skiing, golfing, jogging and driving.
  • What can I choose from? Non-prescription clip-ons or wraparounds that fit over your glasses, prescription “shades,” or glasses that darken in bright light.
  • Anything else? A broad-brimmed hat helps block light that leaks in at the sides or top. If you wear prescription eyewear, you may also want UV-blocking contact lenses.

10 LASIK & surgical co-management

LASIK (laser assisted in situ keratomileusis) is a corrective alternative to glasses or contact lenses for nearsightedness, farsightedness and astigmatism. Our optometrists offer pre- and post-operative care for patients having LASIK or other eye surgery, so you get the clearest vision possible.

  • Evaluation and referral — we review your history and fully assess your eye health and vision to see if you’re a good candidate and if your prescription is stable. If so, we refer you to the LASIK surgery center we’ve worked with on an ongoing basis. The center does advanced testing and discusses your surgical options, and we stay in touch with them about your evaluation.
  • After surgery — we schedule your follow-up visits, typically starting the day after surgery and continuing at set times over the next six months. A full exam is recommended after one year to check the long-term results.
  • Advantages — LASIK can be a great option for people who play sports, have allergies, or want simplicity.

Questions about LASIK? Call your office or email us.
